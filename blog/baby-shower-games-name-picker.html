10 Baby Shower Games That Need a Random Name Picker

Baby showers run on games, and baby shower games run on fairness. A random name picker handles teams, turn order, and prize draws — no favoritism, no awkward silences, no "volunteers" who instantly regret it.

Why Baby Shower Games Need a Name Picker

Every host knows the moment: "Okay, I need two volunteers!" and the room goes silent. Half the guests are shy, the other half are already whispering about who should go. A random name picker deletes that moment entirely. Names go in, the tool picks, and the chosen guest can't argue — it wasn't the host playing favorites, it was fate. It also quietly solves the fairness problem for prizes: a draw everyone watches is a draw nobody disputes.

All ten games below assume one thing: the guest list typed into your phone, one name per line. That's the whole setup.

Team Games (1–4)

1. Baby trivia teams. Split guests into 2–4 teams with a team generator and quiz them: celebrity baby names, nursery rhymes, "how many diapers does a newborn use in a week?" Even teams keep it competitive instead of lopsided.

2. Diaper-changing relay. The classic chaos game — teams race to diaper a doll (or a very patient balloon). Use the name picker to draft teams so nobody can stack their squad with the moms who've done this a thousand times.

3. Nursery rhyme quiz. Read the first line of a rhyme; teams buzz in with the next line. Random teams mean the new parents-to-be aren't all on one side demolishing everyone else.

4. Baby photo guessing teams. Everyone submitted a baby photo; teams guess who's who. Draft the teams randomly so the guest of honor's college friends don't form an unstoppable photo-recognition machine.

Baby Shower Games With Fair Turn Order (5–7)

5. Baby charades order. Acting out "morning sickness" in front of twenty people is brave enough without also volunteering to go first. Let the picker set the performance order — going third because fate said so is much easier than raising your hand.

6. Gift-opening order. If the guest of honor opens gifts during the party, draw names to decide whose gift gets opened when. It spreads the attention around and gives every giver their moment.

7. "The Price Is Right" baby edition. Hold up a baby item; guests guess the price without going over. Use the picker to set the guessing order — going last is a real advantage (you've heard everyone's guesses), so randomizing the order keeps it fair across rounds. Rotate the order each round.

Prize & Winner Games (8–10)

8. Door prize draw. The easiest win on this list: at the end of the shower, paste the guest list into the picker and draw the door prize winner live, with everyone watching. Screenshot it for the group chat.

9. Baby bingo caller. In baby bingo (diaper, bottle, onesie...), have the picker choose who calls the next item — or better, spin a prize wheel loaded with the bingo items themselves to draw the next call. No caller needed at all.

10. Raffle draw. Selling raffle tickets for a big prize? Number the tickets, paste the numbers into a random number generator, and draw the winning number in front of the room. For a charity or diaper-fund raffle, the visible draw is what makes people trust it enough to buy tickets.

How to Run It All From Your Phone

You don't need a laptop or a projector — though a TV screen makes draws extra dramatic. Before the party, save the guest list in your notes app, one name per line. When a game needs teams, order, or a winner, paste the list into the picker and tap. It takes about ten seconds, and guests will start requesting "the picker" for everything by the second game. Pro tip: keep the guest list saved after the party — you'll want it again for the sip-and-see.

One more host trick: run the quiet games (trivia, photo guessing) while guests arrive and mingle, then save the loud ones (relays, charades) for after cake when energy peaks. The picker handles the logistics either way — you just handle the fun. And if the shower doubles as a baby-name brainstorm, a random name generator is a lighthearted way to crowdsource ideas from the whole room.

And if you're the guest of honor reading this? Send this article to your host. Future you, watching a fair draw instead of an awkward volunteer moment, says thanks.

Frequently asked questions

How do you pick baby shower game winners fairly?

Use a random draw with the full guest list visible on screen, done in one take. When everyone watches the pick happen, nobody questions it.

What's a good free name picker for parties?

A free browser-based list randomizer — paste the names, tap once, and it picks. No signup, no app, works on any phone.

How do you split baby shower guests into teams?

Paste the guest names into a team generator, choose how many teams you want, and generate. You get even teams instantly, with zero awkward volunteering.

Can I use a name picker for the door prize draw?

Yes — it's the fairest way to do it. Show the guest list, draw live in front of everyone, and screenshot the result for the announcement.
