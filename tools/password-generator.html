About this password generator

Weak passwords get cracked in seconds; strong ones take centuries. This generator creates passwords using your browser's cryptographic random number generator — the same quality of randomness used in security software — and guarantees at least one character from every character type you select.

Privacy first: everything happens on your device. We never see, store, or transmit your passwords. Once you leave the page, the password exists only where you saved it. Pair every generated password with a password manager (Bitwarden, 1Password, or your browser's built-in one) so you only ever have to remember one master password.

Tips for strong passwords

  • Length beats complexity: use at least 16 characters — every extra character multiplies cracking time.
  • Never reuse passwords: breaches leak passwords constantly, and attackers try stolen passwords on every major site (credential stuffing).
  • Use a password manager: generate a unique random password for every account and let the manager remember them.
  • Enable two-factor authentication (2FA): an authenticator app or security key protects you even if a password leaks.
  • Prefer passphrases when you must memorize: four or more random words beat a short "complex" password.

Frequently asked questions

Is this password generator safe?

Yes. Passwords are generated locally in your browser with cryptographic randomness and never leave your device.

How long should my password be?

At least 16 characters with mixed character types. Longer is always stronger.

Do you store my passwords?

No — nothing is stored, logged or transmitted. Once you leave the page, it's gone.

What is password entropy?

Entropy measures unpredictability in bits. A 16-character password from all character types has around 100+ bits — far beyond what brute-force attacks can crack in any realistic timeframe.

Should I use a passphrase instead?

A passphrase of 4+ random words is a good memorable alternative. For maximum security, use a long random password from this generator plus a password manager.

Why shouldn't I reuse passwords?

Because breaches leak passwords constantly. If one site is compromised and you reused that password, attackers try it everywhere — a technique called credential stuffing.

Do I still need two-factor authentication?

Yes. A strong password plus 2FA (an authenticator app or security key) protects you even if the password itself is somehow exposed.
