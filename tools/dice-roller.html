About this virtual dice roller

Left your dice bag at home? Roll up to six dice with any standard side count — d4, d6, d8, d10, d12 or d20. Each die tumbles independently with cryptographic randomness, totals are added automatically, and special rolls get celebrated: natural 20s, snake eyes, doubles, and maximum rolls all trigger their own fanfare.

The polyhedral dice set is the heart of Dungeons & Dragons and tabletop RPGs: the d20 resolves ability checks, attack rolls, and saving throws, while the smaller dice handle weapon damage and hit points. Board gamers use d6s for classics like Monopoly, Catan, and backgammon. Whatever your game, these virtual dice are arguably fairer than physical ones — no worn edges, no loaded dice.

How to use it

  1. Choose how many dice (1–6) and how many sides (d4–d20).
  2. Press Roll Dice — listen for the rattle.
  3. Read the total, check the quips, and roll again.

Popular uses

  • D&D & RPGs: ability checks, saving throws, damage rolls, and hit dice.
  • Board games: a digital stand-in for lost dice — d6 for Monopoly, Catan, and more.
  • Teaching probability: roll often and graph the distribution; compare 1d12 vs 2d6.
  • Decision games: highest roll goes first.

Frequently asked questions

How do I roll dice online?

Choose how many dice and how many sides (d4 through d20), then press Roll Dice.

Can I roll a d20 for D&D?

Yes — select 1 die and 20 sides for a classic d20 roll, or roll multiple dice for damage.

Are virtual dice rolls fair?

Yes — every face of every die has an equal chance, using cryptographic-quality randomness.

How many dice can I roll at once?

Up to 6 dice per roll, with d4, d6, d8, d10, d12, or d20 sides. Totals are added automatically.

What does "natural 20" mean?

Rolling a 20 on a d20 — a critical success in D&D. Our roller celebrates it with a fanfare and confetti.

Can I use this instead of physical dice for D&D?

Absolutely. Many groups roll digitally, especially online. The roller handles d20 checks, damage dice, and totals instantly.

What's the difference between d6 and 2d6?

One six-sided die (1–6) versus two added together (2–12). Multiple dice cluster around the average — that's why 2d6 feels different from 1d12.
