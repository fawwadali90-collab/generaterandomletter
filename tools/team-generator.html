About this random team generator

Enter any list of names and split them into balanced, random teams. The list is shuffled with cryptographic randomness and dealt round-robin, so teams are as even as possible and nobody can complain about stacked picks or being chosen last.

Random assignment beats "captains pick" every time for casual play: it's faster, it's provably fair, and it mixes friend groups instead of clustering them. Teachers love it for project groups, lab partners, and breakout rooms; coaches use it for pickup games and tournament brackets; offices use it for hackathon teams and Secret Santa pools. Project it on screen and the whole room can see the draw happen.

How to use it

  1. Paste your names, one per line.
  2. Choose how many teams (2–6).
  3. Press Generate Teams — watch them deal in one by one.
  4. Press again anytime to reshuffle into brand-new teams.

Frequently asked questions

How do I split names into random teams?

Paste your names (one per line), choose the number of teams, and press Generate Teams.

Are the teams really random?

Yes — names are shuffled with cryptographic-quality randomness before being dealt evenly into teams.

What if the names don't divide evenly?

Teams are dealt round-robin, so sizes never differ by more than one person.

Can teachers use this for classroom groups?

Yes — it's ideal for project groups, lab partners, debate teams, and breakout groups. Project it so students can see the draw is fair.

Can I make more than 2 teams?

Yes — split into up to 6 teams at once. If you pick more teams than names, the count is capped automatically.

How do I keep teams balanced by skill?

This tool randomizes, which is the fairest for casual play. For skill-balanced teams, seed players by rank first, then randomize within tiers.

What's the fairest way to pick teams?

Random shuffling beats playground picks — no one is chosen last. Read our comparison of 7 team-splitting methods on the blog.
